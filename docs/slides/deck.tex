% ============================================== PART I  THE THESIS

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Expressive performance}
  The score fixes pitches and note values. Performers add the rest:
  when, how long, how loud, and on strings and winds, how the pitch
  moves inside the note. These deviations are the expression.
  \vspace{-2mm}
  \begin{center}
  \includegraphics[height=0.66\textheight]{deck_concept.png}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The thesis, and the three problems}
  \textbf{Thesis: one Gaussian process on the score graph turns a known
  score and a performance into per-note expressive variables, each with
  an error bar you can trust.}
  \vspace{1mm}

  A recording is read as four layers: \textbf{score} (what was
  written), \textbf{expression} (how each note was played: the
  object of inference), \textbf{timbre} (what the instrument adds,
  integrated out), \textbf{noise}.
  \vspace{1mm}

  Three observation problems:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Piano}: timing, articulation, velocity, read directly
          from aligned MIDI. Are the error bars calibrated, note by
          note?
    \item \textbf{Strings and winds}: six variables, now with
          intonation and vibrato, \textbf{estimated} from audio,
          noisily. Does the same prior still work?
    \item \textbf{Audio directly}: no pitch tracker at all. Can the
          waveform itself be the observation?
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Related work}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
  \column{0.50\textwidth}
  \textbf{Expressive performance modelling}
  {\small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item rule systems: KTH performance rules (Friberg et al.\ 2006)
    \item linear basis-function models (Grachten and Widmer 2012)
    \item deep rendering models: VirtuosoNet (Jeong et al.\ 2019)
  \end{itemize}
  All output one rendering or corpus statistics, none a calibrated
  per-note posterior.}
  \column{0.48\textwidth}
  \textbf{Gaussian processes and graphs}
  {\small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item GPs over graphs (Venkitaraman et al.\ 2018)
    \item graph Mat\'ern kernels (Borovitskiy et al.\ 2021)
    \item multi-output coupling, ICM (Bonilla et al.\ 2008)
    \item differentiable synthesis (Engel et al.\ 2020)
  \end{itemize}}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  This work combines them: a graph GP over score positions, with
  per-note side information, for calibrated performance inference, down
  to the waveform.
\end{frame}

\divider{The model}{A multi-output Gaussian process on the score graph}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The model (1): the score graph and its kernel}
  Nodes are the $N$ written notes: pitch $p_i$, beat onset $b_i$, beat
  duration $d_i$. Edges connect notes close in score time and pitch
  (neighbours should be played similarly):
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    W_{ij} \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl( -\tfrac{(b_i - b_j)^2}{2\,\ell_b^2}
                             \;-\; \tfrac{(p_i - p_j)^2}{2\,\ell_p^2} \Bigr),
    \qquad
    \Lg \;=\; D - W,\qquad D_{ii} = \sum_j W_{ij}.
  \end{equation*}
  \vspace{-2mm}
  Eigendecompose the Laplacian, $\Lg = U \diag(\nu)\, U^{\!\top}$, and
  shape its spectrum with one parameter $s$:
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    K_G(s) \;=\; U\, g(\nu; s)\, U^{\!\top},
    \qquad
    g(\nu; s) \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + s\,\nu}
    \quad\text{(graph Mat\'ern, } \alpha = 1\text{)} .
  \end{equation*}
  {\small Smooth-on-the-graph components keep their variance, rough
  ones are shrunk. $\ell_b = 2$ beats, $\ell_p = 4$ semitones, fixed;
  $s$ learned per piece. $W, D, \Lg, K_G \in \R^{N \times N}$.}
  \begin{center}
  \includegraphics[height=0.24\textheight]{deck_scoregraph.png}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The model (2): channels coupled, side information as kernels}
  The channels are correlated, so they share the graph through a learned
  coupling matrix $B$ ($c, c'$ index the channels):
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{Cov}\bigl[\, y_{i,c},\; y_{j,c'} \bigr]
      \;=\; B_{cc'}\, \bigl[K_G(s)\bigr]_{ij}
    \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
    \operatorname{Cov}[\,y\,] \;=\; B \otimes K_G(s).
  \end{equation*}
  \vspace{-2mm}
  An observed channel at one note informs the other channels at
  neighbouring notes.
  \vspace{2mm}

  Per-note side information, one row per note ($N \times d$): score
  descriptors $X_{\mathrm{feat}}$ (register, duration, meter, position)
  and features $H$ from a small music model trained on MAESTRO. A linear
  mean with unknown weights $\beta$, integrated out, is one more kernel
  term ($X$ = either feature matrix, $f_G$ = the graph GP above, $c_X$
  = the prior scale of the weights):
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    y \;=\; X\beta + f_G, \quad \beta \sim \N(0,\, c_X\,I)
    \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
    \operatorname{Cov}[\,y\,] \;=\; c_X\, X X^{\!\top} + \operatorname{Cov}[\,f_G\,].
  \end{equation*}
  {\small The weights are not fitted once across the corpus: they are
  \textbf{re-inferred for every piece}, jointly with everything else,
  and their uncertainty enters the posterior. This per-piece adaptation
  is the main source of the accuracy gain.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The model (3): the covariance of one performance}
  Stack all channels of the $N$ notes into one vector $y$.
  Every component becomes one term of its covariance $K$, built per piece:
  \begin{equation*}
    K \;=\;
    \underbrace{\textcolor{okblue}{B \otimes K_G(s)}}_{\text{coupled graph GP}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{\textcolor{okamber}{\textstyle\sum_f \diag(c_f)\otimes X_f X_f^{\!\top}}}_{\text{side information}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{\textcolor{okverm}{\diag(\varsigma^2)\otimes I}}_{\text{noise}}
  \end{equation*}
  {\small $f$ runs over the two feature sets, giving per-channel scales
  $c_{\mathrm{feat}}, c_{\mathrm{mm}}$. $\varsigma^2$ = the per-channel
  noise variances.}
  \vspace{1mm}

  All parameters $\theta = (s, B, \{c_f\}, \varsigma^2)$, 16 numbers on
  piano (the construction is identical for any channel count), from one
  exact evidence per piece. Prediction is conjugate:
  \begin{equation*}
    m = C_{\cdot o} K_{oo}^{-1} \tilde y_o, \qquad
    \Sigma_y = C - C_{\cdot o} K_{oo}^{-1} C_{o\cdot},
  \end{equation*}
  {\small $\tilde y_o$: the observed values. $o$: the observed entries of
  $y$. $C$: the noise-free part of $K$. $m, \Sigma_y$: posterior mean and
  covariance. No variational approximation anywhere, and the baselines are
  exact special cases (diagonal $B$, zero $c_f$, fixed cross-piece mean).}
\end{frame}

\divider{Piano}{Timing, articulation, velocity from aligned MIDI}

\begin{frame}{Setup: aligned performance MIDI}
  Data: \textbf{ASAP}, score-aligned Disklavier performances. ASAP
  reuses MAESTRO recordings, and the music model pretrains on MAESTRO,
  so every performance seen in pretraining is removed (1036 to 653).
  Development 30 pieces, one-shot test 20 untouched pieces, 400 notes
  per piece (12{,}000 and 8{,}000 notes). Per written note, three
  variables, read directly, no estimation.
  \vspace{1mm}

  Task: hide a fraction of the notes, predict them from the rest, score
  the hidden notes. Headline operating point: 40 percent hidden; the
  sweep runs 50 percent down to leave-one-out.
  \begin{equation*}
    y_i \;=\; \bigl[\;\underbrace{\tau_i}_{\text{onset timing}},\;\;
      \underbrace{\log r_i}_{\text{articulation}},\;\;
      \underbrace{v_i}_{\text{velocity}}\;\bigr]
  \end{equation*}
  \vfill
  Metrics, per channel and per level: RMSE for accuracy, log score and
  coverage for the error bars. Comparisons are paired per piece.
\end{frame}

\divider{Strings and winds}{Intonation, vibrato, loudness, timing, estimated from tracked audio}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Setup: audio instead of MIDI}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Data: \textbf{URMP}, 44 chamber pieces (duets to quintets, 13
          instrument types), each instrument recorded on its own
          monophonic track with note annotations and the score. Split by
          composition, since arrangements share recordings: development
          31 pieces (78 tracks, about 17{,}000 notes), one-shot test 13
          pieces.
    \item Pitch now carries expression: players control intonation and
          vibrato within every note.
    \item A pitch tracker (pYIN) turns each track into frames: time,
          $f_0$, voicing confidence. The variables must be
          \textbf{estimated} from these frames, noisily, with a variance
          per note.
    \item Two measurements of every note exist: our tracked curve, and
          the corpus's own annotation of the same track (cleaner, but
          still a measurement). Evaluation uses the second.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{From the tracked frames to the pitch-curve channels}
  \small
  A pitch tracker (pYIN) turns each track into frames
  $(t_j, f_{0,j}, \pi_j)$: time, fundamental frequency, voicing
  confidence.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frame rule}: note $i$ keeps
          $J_i = \{\, j:\ \text{voiced},\ \pi_j \ge q_{0.2},\
          t_j \in [o_i,\, o_i + u_i) \,\}$, at least 4 frames
          ($o_i, u_i$: annotated onset and duration; $q_{0.2}$: the
          track's lowest confidence quintile).
    \item \textbf{Cents curve}: each kept frame becomes a deviation from
          equal temperament at 440 Hz,
          $x_j = 1200 \log_2 (f_{0,j}/440) - 100\,(p_i - 69)$
          ($p_i$: MIDI pitch).
    \item \textbf{Channels}: within one note, a sustained level $c$ plus
          one oscillation of extent $\gamma$ and rate $f$; the model
          receives $c$, $\log\gamma$, $\log f$ ($\gamma$ and $f$ are
          positive, so on the log scale).
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{center}
  \includegraphics[height=0.40\textheight]{channels_def.png}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Estimating the channels with a Gaussian process}
  The note's cents curve as a \textbf{Gaussian process}, two components:
  a damped cosine at the vibrato rate, plus a slow drift (a special case
  of the spectral mixture kernel, Wilson and Adams 2013):
  \begin{equation*}
    k(\Delta t) \;=\;
    \underbrace{w_1\, e^{-2\pi^2 v_1 \Delta t^2} \cos(2\pi \mu_1 \Delta t)}_{\text{vibrato component}}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{w_2\, e^{-2\pi^2 v_2 \Delta t^2}}_{\text{drift component}}
  \end{equation*}
  {\small $\Delta t$: time lag between two frames (s), so $\tau$ stays
  the timing channel. $\mu_1$: rate (Hz). $w_1, w_2$: powers (cents$^2$).
  $v_1, v_2$: bandwidths (Hz$^2$).}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The prior states only: the curve's energy sits in a band of
          width $\sqrt{v_1}$ around the rate $\mu_1$, plus a
          low-frequency drift band around zero. Nothing else is assumed.
    \item Fit per note by exact marginal likelihood, variances from
          the evidence curvature.
    \item Channels from the posterior: $c$ the realized average,
          $\gamma$ from the vibrato component, $f = \mu_1$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\divider{The waveform as the observation}{Methodology and first development studies, no claims}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The method, the data and the signal model}
  \small
  \textbf{Takes} from DDSP (Engel et al.\ 2020): gradient descent
  through a differentiable synthesizer. \textbf{Keeps}: the exact
  amplitude-marginalized likelihood and the GP priors, no neural
  encoder, no spectral surrogate loss. \textbf{Measured}: a learned
  many-component spectrum adds nothing over the hand structure.
  \textbf{Data}: URMP development tracks, audio only. 7 tracks, one per
  instrument, notes cut at annotated boundaries, 16\,kHz; 376 notes in
  the curve studies, 47 chains of 3 to 5 notes (154 boundaries) in the
  boundary study.
  \vspace{2mm}

  A sustained note is a stack of $K$ harmonics. Its audio is
  \begin{equation*}
    x \;=\; \Phi(z)\,a \;+\; \varepsilon,
    \qquad x \in \mathbb R^{m},\quad \Phi(z) \in \mathbb R^{m\times p},\quad
    a \in \mathbb R^{p},\quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal N(0,\ \sigma^2 I_m)
  \end{equation*}
  \vspace{-2mm}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    $x$ & the $m$ recorded samples \\
    $z$ & the pitch curve, the \textbf{unknown} we are after \\
    $\Phi(z)$ & one column per harmonic wave; the curve fixes every column's \textbf{shape} \\
    $a$ & the harmonic amplitudes, the timbre; they only \textbf{scale} the columns \\
    $\varepsilon$ & what the harmonics do not explain
  \end{tabular}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{From the pitch curve to the likelihood: the timbre integrated out}
  \small
  For any candidate curve, every harmonic's wave follows exactly
  (frequency is the speed, phase the distance travelled, so a small
  pitch error grows into a large phase error):
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{alignat*}{2}
    &\text{pitch curve}\qquad & z &= \text{cents}(t),\qquad
       f_0(t) = f_{\text{pitch}}\cdot 2^{\,\text{cents}(t)/1200}\\[0.5mm]
    &\text{phase, design} & \varphi_k(t) &= 2\pi k \int_0^{t} f_0,\qquad
       \Phi(z) = \bigl[\cos\varphi_k(t),\ \sin\varphi_k(t)\bigr]_{k=1..K}
       \ \in \mathbb R^{m\times p}
  \end{alignat*}
  \vspace{-3mm}

  The amplitudes are unknown but \textbf{linear}. A Gaussian prior on
  them integrates out exactly:
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    a \sim \mathcal N(0,\ \sigma_a^2 I_p)
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    p(x \mid z) \;=\; \mathcal N\!\bigl(x;\ 0,\ \sigma_a^2\,\Phi(z)\Phi(z)^{\!\top} + \sigma^2 I_m\bigr)
  \end{equation*}
  \vspace{-3mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item One number per candidate curve, timbre-free. Every audio
          sample votes. No pitch tracker anywhere.
    \item Computed through the $p \times p$ matrix
          $G = \Phi^{\!\top}\Phi + \tfrac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_a^2} I_p$,
          never the $m \times m$ covariance: cost $O(mp^2)$, $p \ll m$.
    \item $\sigma^2$: the residual variance at the best amplitudes,
          fitted per note. The curve is held at $J$ knot values and
          interpolated; columns repeat per time chunk, $p = 2KC$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Finding the curve, with its uncertainty}
  \small
  \begin{alignat*}{2}
    &\text{prior on the curve} \qquad & u &\sim \mathcal N(0,\ K_{\text{knots}})\\[1mm]
    &\text{best curve} & \hat z &= \arg\max_z\ \bigl[\log p(x \mid z) + \log p(z)\bigr]\\[1mm]
    &\text{its band} & \Sigma_z &\approx
       \Bigl[-\nabla_z^2\bigl(\log p(x \mid z) + \log p(z)\bigr)\Bigr]^{-1}_{\hat z}
  \end{alignat*}
  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The score anchors $f_{\text{pitch}}$; $K_{\text{knots}}$ is the
          two-component GP of the previous chapter (vibrato band plus
          drift), so candidates are curves a player would produce.
    \item $\Phi(z)$ is differentiable in $z$: gradient descent through
          the synthesizer (cf.\ Engel et al.\ 2020), seconds per note.
    \item The band is the Laplace approximation: a sharply curved peak
          gives a narrow band.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

          a seventh channel: held-out intonation improves on 14 of 14
          track and seed pairs ($-1.36$ cents vs the six-channel bundle,
          significant), and the empirical calibration floor halves
          (3.1 to 1.8 cents).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Note boundaries: the chain model}
  \small
  A chain of $N$ consecutive notes, boundaries $b_1, \ldots, b_{N-1}$,
  one pitch curve $z_k$ per note. Each note is switched on and off by a
  soft window:
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{alignat*}{2}
    &\text{window of note } k \qquad & w_k(t) &= \sigma\!\Bigl(\tfrac{t - b_{k-1}}{s}\Bigr)\,
       \sigma\!\Bigl(\tfrac{b_k - t}{s}\Bigr),
       \qquad \sigma(u) = \tfrac{1}{1 + e^{-u}},\quad s = 10\,\text{ms}\\[1mm]
    &\text{chain design} & \Phi(z, b) &= \bigl[\,w_1 \odot \Phi_1(z_1)\ \big|\ \cdots\ \big|\ w_N \odot \Phi_N(z_N)\,\bigr]
       \ \in \mathbb R^{m \times Np}\\[1mm]
    &\text{likelihood, as before} & p(x \mid z, b) &= \mathcal N\!\bigl(x;\ 0,\ \sigma_a^2\,\Phi\Phi^{\!\top} + \sigma^2 I_m\bigr)\\[1mm]
    &\text{start} & b^{(0)} &= \text{the score through the local tempo line (no annotation)}\\[1mm]
    &\text{joint fit} \qquad & (\hat z, \hat b) &= \arg\max_{z,\,b}\ \bigl[\log p(x \mid z, b) + \log p(z)\bigr],
       \qquad \text{bands: Laplace over } (z, b)
  \end{alignat*}
  \vspace{-2mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $w_k$ fades note $k$ in after $b_{k-1}$, out at $b_k$ ($\odot$:
          columns scaled sample by sample). Differentiable in $b$: the
          boundaries move by the same gradient descent as the curves.
    \item The audio decides: a boundary settles where note $k$'s
          harmonics stop explaining the samples and note $k{+}1$'s start.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The score prior inside the waveform inference: three spans}
  \small
  One latent per chain element, $\theta \in \R^{n}$ (intonation constants
  $c$ in cents, or joint offsets $b$ in ms); three priors at matched
  marginal variance, every arm with the common-mode scalar $t$:
  \vspace{-2mm}
  \begin{align*}
    \theta &= t\mathbf{1} + \tilde\theta,\qquad t \sim \N(0,\sigma_t^2),\qquad
    \tilde\theta \sim \N(0,\sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{arm}}),\qquad
    K = \sigma^2 K_{\mathrm{arm}} + \sigma_t^2 \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}\\
    K_{\mathrm{ind}} &= I,\qquad
    K_{\mathrm{arm}} = \diag(M)^{-\frac12} M \diag(M)^{-\frac12},\qquad
    M = (I + L_{\mathrm{arm}})^{-1}
  \end{align*}
  \vspace{-5mm}

  $L_{\mathrm{arm}}$: Laplacian of the note-order chain (temporal)
  or of the score graph (time and pitch). The hidden element $h$ is
  dropped from the design, so its posterior is the prior conditional,
  scored against the reference centre:
  \vspace{-2mm}
  \begin{equation*}
    \theta_h \mid \theta_{-h} \sim \N\big(K_{h,-h} K_{-h,-h}^{-1}
    \theta_{-h},\; K_{hh} - K_{h,-h} K_{-h,-h}^{-1} K_{-h,h}\big).
  \end{equation*}
  \vspace{-5mm}
  \begin{center}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
    \toprule
    span (development) & hidden element & graph $-$ independent & graph $-$ temporal \\
    \midrule
    47 chains, 3 to 5 notes & one note's $c$ (cents) & $-0.02$ $[-0.33,+0.32]$ & $-0.19$ $[-0.89,+0.46]$ \\
    40 joints & one joint's $b$ (ms) & $-2.45$ $[-8.38,+3.32]$ & $+1.20$ $[-2.12,+4.45]$ \\
    48 phrases, 6 to 10 notes & a recurring pitch's $c$ (cents) & $-0.07$ $[-0.42,+0.27]$ & $-1.63$ $[-2.93,-0.46]^{*}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-2mm}
  Every prior transfers the common offset (about 7 cents, 10 to
  14 ms against the no-transfer references). The score graph is never
  worse than the temporal chain and repairs its harm at phrase scale
  (temporal $-$ independent $+1.56^{*}$); beyond the offset, nothing
  measurable so far.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What in the graph does the work: the time window}
  The score graph's edge weight, and the two graphs it is set against
  in the same GP (same features, same per-piece intercept, only $W$
  changes):
  \vspace{-1mm}
  \begin{align*}
    \text{score graph: }& W_{ij} = \exp\!\Bigl( -\tfrac{(b_i - b_j)^2}{2\,\ell_b^2}
                          -\tfrac{(p_i - p_j)^2}{2\,\ell_p^2} \Bigr),\qquad
    \ell_b = 2 \text{ beats},\ \ell_p = 4 \text{ semitones}\\
    \text{time Gaussian: }& \text{the same with } \ell_p \to \infty;\qquad
    \text{time chain: } W_{ij} = \mathbb{1}[\,|i-j| = 1\,].
  \end{align*}
  \vspace{-4mm}
  \begin{center}
  \footnotesize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
    \toprule
    setting (development) & channel & graph $-$ chain & graph $-$ time Gaussian \\
    \midrule
    strings and winds, 78 tracks & intonation RMSE (cents) & $-0.50$ $[-0.67,-0.34]^{*}$ & $-0.02$ $[-0.10,+0.06]$ \\
    153 track-and-seed cells & vibrato extent / rate RMSE & $-0.22^{*}$ / $-0.21^{*}$ & $+0.04^{*}$ / $+0.03^{*}$ \\
    piano, 30 pieces, 4 seeds & pooled RMSE / log score & & $+0.008^{*}$ / $+0.019^{*}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-1mm}
  {\small Negative favours the first arm; $*$: the 95 percent interval
  excludes zero. On both corpora the score graph beats the chain and
  equals, or slightly trails, the same graph with the pitch term removed.
  The structure that does the work is smoothness in score time over
  about two beats; pitch proximity adds nothing measurable. The
  confirmed results stand (full model against its alternatives); what
  narrows is the attribution of the graph term.\par}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
  One Gaussian process on the score graph, three observation types:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Piano}: beats the two-stage baseline on accuracy and
          calibration, confirmed once on untouched pieces.
    \item \textbf{Strings and winds}: the graph improves recovery and
          calibration on the estimated channels, confirmed once on
          held-out pieces.
    \item \textbf{Audio}: methodology in place and running at
          development level --- curves, boundaries, the timbre
          read-out, the score prior inside the inference at three
          spans --- no claims yet; the joint loop over a whole
          performance is the direction ahead.
    \item \textbf{What the graph does}: at every scale tested, on both
          corpora, its value is smoothing in score time over about two
          beats; pitch proximity adds nothing measurable (development).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
