## 1. Harmonic-edge question: CLOSED on development evidence

The redundancy tie ("harmonic edges add nothing once the music-model
embeddings are in the kernel") was anchor-rate-only. Running `c_harm_lm` vs
`b_featlm` at every masking level gives a **density gradient**:

| hidden | dRMSE (paired) | NLL |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | −0.004 ns (+ one fit collapse, see below) | ns |
| 40% (operating point) | tie (adoption record) | tie |
| 30% | **−0.0096\*** | ns, 19/30 better |
| 20% | **−0.0105\*** | ns, 21/30 better |
| 10% | **−0.0089\*** | ns, 22/30 better |

Reading: extra edge weights are per-piece hyperparameters — dense
observation determines them and they pay; sparse observation leaves them
under-determined and, at 50% hidden, fragile (one guard-invisible fit
collapse from degenerate learned length scales — a new demonstrated instance
of the documented safeguard blind spot). **Decision: no adoption; the thesis
keeps the plain graph at its operating point**; harmonic edges recorded as a
measured dense-observation refinement. No second confirmation set spent.
(Thesis §kernels updated; `docs/kernel_multirate_results.md` closed.)

## 4. NEW: Phase 2 end-to-end on real audio (development, 78 unique tracks)

The full specified pipeline ran end to end on real recordings for the first
time: pyin f0 → confidence-filtered per-note NLLS targets ([c, log γ,
log f], delta-method variances, identifiability → genuine per-(note,channel)
missingness at 55–93% identifiable per track) → heteroscedastic cell-mask
graph GP → held-out imputation (30% of notes hidden, 2 seeds, 77 unique
tracks after deduplicating shared recordings).

**The graph prior wins on every channel of the four-channel audio bundle,
on both axes, on real audio** (paired per-(track,seed) vs the no-graph
ablation, dev; loudness ℓ = per-note log RMS joined the bundle):

| channel | graph value (dRMSE) | coverage@90 (as-given GP) |
|---|---|---|
| intonation c | **−0.92 cents [−1.50, −0.43]\*** | 0.89 |
| vibrato extent log γ | **−0.15 [−0.23, −0.08]\*** | 0.91 |
| vibrato rate log f | **−0.17 [−0.23, −0.11]\*** | 0.88 |
| loudness ℓ | **−0.010 [−0.015, −0.005]\*** | 0.91 |

And the win holds **within every instrument family**: strings, woodwind, and
brass each show significant graph value on intonation and both vibrato
channels (woodwinds largest on intonation, −2.4 cents; the only wash is
loudness on woodwinds). Pooled RMSE: c 14.0 vs 14.4 cents; medians (11.2 vs
12.3) confirm no cell hides in the average. Scoring against
ground-truth-derived targets (the same NLLS on URMP's GT F0 curves —
quasi-truth, independent of the tracker) gives the same ordering on every
channel. Estimator-missing vibrato cells on visible notes are filled by the
GP — the capability no per-note method has (figure
`phase2_real_example.png`).

**And the τ policy has a measured answer** (`results/tau_feasibility_dev.md`):
URMP's annotated onsets anchor a score-time warp on 76/78 development tracks
with **no audio aligner** (half by exact order match, half via pitch DTW).
The local-warp residual τ has median std 79 ms — Phase-1-scale timing —
with lag-1 neighbour correlation +0.59, exactly the structure the graph
prior models. Timing can be a Phase-2 channel on this corpus.

Two honest findings along the way:
- **A GT-validated estimator-failure rule**: 2.2% of intonation targets are
  octave slips (|c| ≈ 1200); the GT cross-check is unanimous (0/358 agree
  with truth), so they are marked missing — a target-quality rule with
  measured justification, not eval tuning.
- **The pilot's noise decision re-confirmed on real data**: with the failure
  cells present, the learned noise scale collapses catastrophically while
  as-given stays healthy; after the rule, as-given still has the best
  calibration against quasi-truth (0.83–0.86 vs 0.74–0.80). As-given
  remains the default.

Per the prereg design this is development evidence: any confirmed Phase-2
claim still requires the frozen, untouched 13-piece confirmation pool after
registration.
