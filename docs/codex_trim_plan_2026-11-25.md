# Whole-thesis audit and trim plan

The thesis statement needs a narrower account of calibration and of the time-window comparison. I would use this wording. **On held-out piano pieces a score-conditioned multi-output Gaussian process improves masked-note recovery and supplies predictive intervals close to nominal coverage. On held-out strings-and-winds recordings the same prior improves intonation recovery and vibrato log scores against estimator targets. Development ablations support a short temporal kernel on piano and on tracked intonation. Annotated motif returns add articulation information across performances of the same movements. In waveform inference the phrase graph improves on a temporal path, and none of the three spans shows measurable improvement beyond a shared offset.** Most of the extra length comes from describing the same evaluations in the introduction, results, Discussion, Future Work and appendices, and from full-page illustrations of results already tabulated. Both proposed editions keep the evidence for these distinctions.

## 1. Findings in severity order

Source locations refer to checkout `9e6b256`. In this report **draft** means `docs/thesis/draft.tex`. A quoted passage identifies the text to revise. The italic sentence is proposed thesis prose. All estimates below come from existing records. Section numbers and printed pages refer to the current edition.

1. **The piano test is judged against a different rule in the methods.** Draft:1294–1299 says the criterion “requires improvement on both recovery (RMSE) and calibration ... against the independent, temporal-only, ridge and music-model-mean-only baselines”. Draft:1318–1332 omits pooled NLL from the original two-stage prediction and gives no numerical interval for any of the three contrasts. The design says “holds on RMSE with NLL not significantly worse” was sufficient for adoption if the within-model graph NLL test also passed (`docs/graphgp_first_design.md:117–123`). The verdict gives the three intervals (`logs/confirmation_verdict.log:11–14`). *Against the two-stage pipeline the paired RMSE difference is −0.0137 [−0.0246, −0.0040] and the pooled NLL difference is +0.0109 [−0.1289, +0.2452]. Within the proposed model the graph improves NLL by 0.0736, with a paired difference of −0.0736 [−0.0918, −0.0574]. These results meet the adoption rule, and coverage is 0.925.*

2. **The protocol assigns the wrong unit of uncertainty to whole studies.** Draft:1249–1255 says “Every comparison ... paired at the level of the piece”. Draft:2146–2148 calls the strings-and-winds interval a “per-piece bootstrap”. The tagged `phase2-registration-2026-08-17:docs/phase2_prereg_design.md:113` specifies “per (track, seed)”. The main table has 78–80 such pairs from 40 tracks on 13 pieces representing seven compositions. The noise follow-up clusters over tracks, the waveform test resamples 48 phrases, and the motif test resamples 18 movements. Repeated measurements on a track are distinct entries in the main URMP bootstrap. See `results/phase2_confirmation_results.md:40–49`, `results/corrnoise_confirmation_results.md:16`, `results/phrase_prior_confirmation_results.md:39` and draft:3455–3459. *The piano and motif intervals resample pieces and movements respectively. The main strings-and-winds and tonal comparisons resample track and mask-seed pairs, the correlated-noise comparison resamples tracks, and the waveform comparison resamples paired phrases.*

3. **The waveform chapter overstates the independence of the boundary inputs.** Draft:2761–2765 says “no access to the annotation anywhere”, and draft:2804 calls the demonstration “the score and the waveform alone”. The result record uses the same shorthand (`results/phase3_chain_dev.md:57–61`), but the evaluator selects chains from annotated onsets and durations, crops audio at annotated endpoints, and sets an interior start to annotated onset minus the stored timing residual when finite (`scripts/eval_phase3_chain_dev.py:78–91,103–110,185–188,216`). Draft:2081–2098 defines that residual from neighbouring performed onsets. The result is boundary refinement given a local alignment. *On annotation-aligned development excerpts the joint fit refines tempo-line estimates of the interior boundaries, reducing median boundary error from 39.4 to 19.5 ms.*

4. **Annotated onsets also enter the URMP predictor.** Figure 7.3 at draft:2343–2346 says “Audio and the annotated onsets enter only to derive the per-note entries”. In the evaluator the same onsets and durations enter the score object, then the graph and the hand-built features (`scripts/eval_phase2_real.py:468–470,483–485`). Draft:2109–2112 states this correctly in the methods. Table 3.1 at draft:651–652 also labels the onset residual as piano-only although draft:2081–2104 defines it for URMP. *On URMP the model uses annotated performed onsets both to construct the graph in seconds and to derive the timing residual. Timing recovery is conditional on that alignment.*

5. **The waveform fusion experiment supplies audio information at the target notes.** Draft:2625–2632 describes “held-out intonation recovery ... on every track-seed pair” without saying what remained visible. The record says “observes the wave channel at held-out notes” (`results/phase3_integration_dev.md:40–45`). Draft:2667–2679 meanwhile says “every note’s expressive variables ... are inferred jointly” and “only the likelihood changes”. The chain, fusion and masked-prior studies have different inputs. The whole-performance joint loop remains open at draft:3738–3751. *In the seven-track development study the waveform measurement is available at the target notes while their original bundle entries are hidden. Adding that measurement reduces intonation RMSE against estimator targets by 1.21 cents. Whole-performance inference under a joint waveform prior remains open.*

6. **Near-nominal coverage is supported for specified targets.** The abstract says all three observation types recover expression “together with a calibrated statement of confidence” (draft:104–108). At draft:2620 the discrepancy floor “restores calibration by construction”. For URMP the given-variance vibrato coverages against estimator targets are 0.89/0.89 and against reference-derived values 0.80/0.85 (`results/phase2_confirmation_results.md:10,18`). Waveform centre coverage is 0.01–0.03 on real references and 0.90 on model-true synthetics. Adding the scalar fusion floor moves reference coverage from 0.77 to 0.80 (`results/phase3_waveform_dev.md:72–77`, `results/phase3_integration_dev.md:9–11`). *The piano and tracked-audio evaluations support predictive coverage for their stated targets. The waveform posterior estimates pitch accurately at the median, but its native bands under-cover the reference.*

7. **Two timing intervals come from different noise arms.** Draft:2270–2272 assigns “+0.003 [+0.000, +0.007]” to the reported 65-ms given-variance system. The learned-scale row has that printed interval at `results/phase2_confirmation_results.md:37`; the given-variance row has “+0.003 [+0.001, +0.007]” at line 48. The record’s narrative at line 81 repeats the other arm’s endpoint. The adverse direction stands. *Under the given variances the graph increases timing RMSE by 0.003 s [0.001, 0.007], and its timing log-score interval includes zero.*

8. **The fit safeguard is internal validation, with observations shared at hyperparameter fitting time.** Draft:1020–1023 says it “screens the fit on data it did not use”. The code first calls `self.fit(Y, mask, ...)`, then constructs the smaller conditioning mask and calls `self.posterior(Y, fit_mask, x)` with those fitted parameters (`src/score_bundle/gp.py:647–655,666–673`). The outer test remains held out. *The safeguard fits hyperparameters to the observed notes and screens predictions made by conditioning on a subset of those notes.*

9. **The curve comparison mixes two arms and two reference quantities.** Draft:2716–2718 pairs “3.2 cents against ... 4.1” with “paired −0.46, better on 56%”. The fitted arm has median frame RMSE 3.16, the fixed arm 3.69, and the reported paired −0.458 and 56% belong to the fixed arm (`results/phase3_curve_dev.md:15–20`). Draft:3884–3886 compares curves against the “reference centre”, although they are scored against reference frames. *Median error against reference pitch frames is 3.16 cents for the fitted-prior curve and 4.09 cents for the tracker. The fixed-prior curve improves on the tracker on 56% of notes.*

10. **The piano robustness remedy was tested on a development analogue.** Draft:1671–1674 says Student-t noise removes “exactly this tail”, and draft:3661–3666 links 105.2 to the held-out example at draft:1656–1662 (+27.5). The separate held-out outlier was evaluated under the original Gaussian model.*

11. **Changing the boundary prior width changes point estimates.** Draft:2972–2976 says the narrower width brings coverage to 0.88 and 0.75 “with the point estimates unchanged”. Median masked boundary error in the three arms moves from 30.6/29.1/28.1 ms to 32.1/33.6/38.6 ms. The three priors still show no clear difference in paired error.*

12. **The completion failure is a mean over pieces presented as a single case.** Draft:1768–1771 says “one evaluation ... reached an RMSE of 1.6 × 10⁴”. The aggregate row prints 16009.3437 (`logs/downstream_gpfirst_report.log:42`), and the breakdown gives “16009 / 0.478 / 480266” as mean, median and worst, with a random-mask extreme as well (`results/completion_fallback_dev.md:30–38`). Weakly determined feature weights also cause failures under random masking.*

13. **Adjacent-note drift correlations cannot identify the cause of held-out coverage.** Draft:2049–2058 calls the discarded within-note variation “graph-white” and says the residual noise scale is “why calibration held”. The reported drift statistic is lag-one correlation along note order, +0.03 on 55 tracks, and the held-out coverage comparison did not vary drift handling (`results/phase2_drift_dev.md:47–51`, `results/phase2_confirmation_results.md:68–73`). *Drift slopes have little correlation between consecutive notes. The available comparisons leave their association with other graph neighbours and their contribution to coverage unresolved.*

14. **The unconditional piano ablation ordering is false.** Draft:4102–4105 puts the proposed model “below the no-graph ablation and that below the features-only model ... at every level”. At 40% hidden the values are 0.3601, 0.3755 and 0.3683 in that order. At 10% hidden the two ablations reverse order. The underlying report claims only that the full model has the lowest RMSE throughout (`docs/masking_sweep_results.md:40–47,70–72`). *The proposed model has the lowest pooled RMSE at every masking level examined. The ordering of the two ablations changes with masking density.*

15. **The development seed comparison overstates agreement.** Draft:2210–2215 says the fresh masks reproduce “every significant recovery difference to two decimals” and describes the initial −3.8 as “on the first seed”. The two initial seeds give −0.891 and −0.256 on intonation and extent, the next two give −0.898 and −0.254. The initial −3.772 extent NLL uses both original seeds (`results/phase2_real_results.md:1,42–44`, `results/phase2_seeds23_dev.md:1,42–46`). *The recovery contrasts are similar across the two pairs of seeds. On vibrato extent the log-score difference is −3.772 on the original seeds and −0.224 on the next two, whose interval includes zero.*

The recurring pitch ablation is on development and tracked observations. *On 77 evaluated development tracks the time-only Gaussian and the full graph give similar intonation gains over the graph-free model. The held-out comparison supports the full graph against the graph-free model.*

The graph improves held-out log score and also contributes to recovery. Its posterior correlation with the embedding component is small on average.*
