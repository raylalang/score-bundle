## 2. New: exact decomposition of the posterior by prior component

Continuing the discussion about understanding each component's effect on the
expressive variables: the GP-first covariance is a sum of independent prior
components (coupled graph GP, score-feature kernel, LM-feature kernel, noise),
and this makes the posterior mean decompose **exactly** — no approximation, no
refitting, no extra model:

    m  =  K_ao (K_oo + Σ_e)^{-1} y_obs
       =  m_graph + m_scorefeat + m_LMfeat,     m_c = K_ao^{(c)} (K_oo + Σ_e)^{-1} y_obs

because the cross-covariance `K_ao` is itself the sum of the component
kernels while the weight vector is shared. Each term is the posterior mean
`E[f_c | y_obs]` of that component (the functional-ANOVA / additive-GP
decomposition). Observation noise shapes the shared weights but has no mean
term of its own at held-out notes.

**Implemented today** as `MultiOutputGraphGP.posterior_components`
(`src/score_bundle/gp.py`), on both the note-mask and Phase-2 cell-mask
paths, with tests asserting the components match an independent brute-force
construction and sum to the published posterior mean (atol 1e-8); the
published code paths are untouched (full suite green).

**Attribution numbers (dev):** per-channel covariance shares of the posterior
mean at held-out notes, `share_c = Cov(m_c, m)/Var(m)` (sums to 1 across
components), aggregated over the 30 dev pieces × 4 anchor-mask seeds of the
published protocol, config `b_featlm`:

| component | τ | log r | v |
|---|---|---|---|
| graph GP | +0.116 ± 0.258 | +0.217 ± 0.216 | +0.265 ± 0.200 |
| score features | +0.692 ± 0.375 | +0.598 ± 0.307 | +0.398 ± 0.182 |
| LM features | +0.192 ± 0.311 | +0.185 ± 0.296 | +0.337 ± 0.195 |

Reading: the score-feature kernel carries most of the posterior *mean* on
every channel; the LM features matter most on velocity (matching the earlier
finding that the LM's edge lives in the `v` channel); the graph's share of
the mean is modest and grows from τ to v. This is consistent with the
adopted attribution story — per-piece Bayesian feature weighting recovers
the mean, while the graph's main value is calibration (covariance), which a
mean-share metric deliberately does not capture. Per-piece spread is large
(the ± values), so per-piece attribution maps are informative on their own.

Figure: `docs/thesis/figures/posterior_components_dev.png` — one dev piece
with the posterior mean split into the three component curves per channel.

**Caveat to state up front:** the split is the evidence-fitted model's own
attribution — it depends on the learned scales (`B`, `c_f`, `ς²`), so where
graph and features could explain the same variation, the per-piece evidence
arbitrates. It is a description of how the fitted model divides labor, not a
causal claim.

**The covariance decomposes too — first redundancy numbers.** The posterior
cross-covariance between components is exact as well, and negative where two
components explain the same variation (explaining-away). Mean per-note
posterior correlation at held-out notes (30 dev pieces, seed 0,
`scripts/eval_component_redundancy.py`, `results/component_redundancy_dev.md`):

| pair | τ | log r | v |
|---|---|---|---|
| graph × LM features | −0.004 | −0.025 | −0.059 |
| score × LM features | −0.067 | −0.079 | −0.183 |
| graph × score features | −0.041 | −0.185 | −0.179 |

Reading: at the fitted model, the graph and the LM components operate almost
**orthogonally** per note — the LM is not doing the graph's job. The real
overlaps are between the two feature kernels (score vs LM, strongest on
velocity) and between graph and score features on articulation/velocity.
This refines the redundancy conversation: the earlier "harmonic edges
redundant under LM-in-kernel" tie is about *extra tonal edges* adding nothing
— next week's `c_harm_lm` run can measure the same correlation on the
harmonic graph directly and show *where* the harmonic edges' information
already lives. (Same ARD caveat as above; script-level for now, to be
promoted into `gp.py` with tests before any thesis-facing use.)
